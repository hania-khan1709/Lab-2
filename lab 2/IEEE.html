1 Department of Cybersecurity, National University of Sciences and Technology, Islamabad, Pakistan

Abstract
This paper presents a comprehensive study of quantum computing applications applied to modern cryptographic architectures and security. We investigate the performance characteristics of various post-quantum algorithms including lattice-based encryption, code-based cryptography, and multivariate public key schemes in high-throughput network environments.
Keywords: Quantum Computing, Post-Quantum Cryptography, Lattice Encryption, Shor's Algorithm, Information Security
I. Introduction

The rapid advancement in quantum computing hardware and fault-tolerant qubit architecture has created unprecedented challenges in securing public key infrastructure and digital signatures [1]. Traditional asymmetric cryptography approaches cannot survive the processing capabilities of large-scale quantum computers [2].

Post-quantum cryptography has emerged as a critical field for securing confidential data against future decryption capabilities. This paper addresses the challenge of implementing post-quantum cryptographic standards within strict network bandwidth constraints [3].

II. Literature Review

Recent advances in quantum security have focused on developing mathematical algorithms that remain intractable for quantum computers. Shor et al. [4] introduced polynomial-time quantum algorithms for factoring integers and solving discrete logarithms.

Lattice-based cryptographic frameworks such as CRYSTALS-Kyber and Dilithium have enabled robust defense against quantum attacks [5]. However, key sizes and memory overhead in constrained hardware environments remain active research areas.

III. Methodology
A. Key Exchange Architecture

Our system implements a three-tier architecture consisting of key generation, encapsulation mechanism, and secure channel setup. Keys are generated across nodes and distributed across securely authenticated channels.

B. Adaptive Key Resynchronization

We implement a sliding window key exchange approach to balance throughput and session security. Session keys are refreshed periodically or when channel instability is detected.

C. Evaluation Metrics

Performance is measured across three dimensions: key generation latency, throughput overhead, and signature validation speed.

IV. Results

Our experiments compared three approaches: legacy RSA-2048, standard lattice encryption, and optimized hybrid post-quantum key exchange. The hybrid approach achieved improved key generation speed while maintaining maximum quantum resistance.

V. Conclusion and Future Work

This work demonstrates that post-quantum lattice-based strategies combined with optimized key exchange architectures can effectively secure high-throughput networks. Future work will explore quantum key distribution (QKD) integration for IoT hardware and lightweight cryptographic primitives for embedded system security.
